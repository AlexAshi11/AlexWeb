01 · ROBOTICS + COMPUTER VISION

Autonomous Raspberry Harvesting & Sorting Robot

Designed and prototyped a complete mechatronic system able to grasp delicate raspberries, detect ripeness, estimate size and route fruit into the correct compartment.

Computer VisionHSV SegmentationArduinoCAD3D Printing
Technical deep dive +

Mechanical design. Rack-and-pinion mechanisms converted servo rotation into controlled linear motion for both gripping and sorting. A servo-actuated rotating gate handled fruit release, while PETG enabled fast iteration of complex parts.

Vision pipeline. A camera classified ripeness through HSV colour segmentation. For ripe fruit, red-pixel count was used to estimate size; the resulting classification was sent to the Arduino to actuate the sorting mechanism.

Engineering outcome. The sensing and decision pipeline was fast and reliable during the competition, and the sorting mechanism consistently positioned the collection box. The project also exposed real design trade-offs: friction/alignment in the drop gate and reduced throughput when handling fruit clusters.

02 · COMPUTATIONAL MECHANICS

Carbon-Fibre Bicycle Frame — FEM

Built a CATIA/Abaqus finite-element workflow to evaluate a laminated carbon bicycle frame under rider weight, cornering loads and modal excitation.

AbaqusCATIA V5Composite PliesMesh ConvergenceModal Analysis
Technical deep dive +

Model. Defined composite behaviour, boundary conditions, load transfer through coupling constraints and multiple static load cases. Critical regions were identified around the bottom bracket, seat tube, head tube and tube junctions.

Numerical verification. Performed progressive global/local mesh refinement and tracked displacement, strain energy and Von Mises stress. Final refinement differences were below 5%, supporting numerical convergence.

Dynamics. Computed natural frequencies and mode shapes, identifying global bending and torsional modes and evaluating resonance risk under normal riding conditions.

03 · STRUCTURAL OPTIMIZATION + VALIDATION

Spaghetti Tower Structural Challenge

Combined material characterization, Abaqus modelling, buckling analysis and physical testing to maximize load-carrying efficiency with minimal structural mass.

Structural OptimizationBucklingWeibullModal AnalysisExperimental Validation
Technical deep dive +

Material characterization. Measured geometry, density, Young’s modulus and failure behaviour using tensile and three-point bending experiments, including Weibull analysis, then transferred the measured properties into Abaqus.

Optimization. Compared geometries and local reinforcement strategies using static, buckling and modal simulations. The final unreinforced non-converging geometry provided the best balance of stiffness, stability and mass.

Validation. Compression testing on a Zwick Roell machine produced a maximum load of 13.01 N at 5.04 mm displacement for an 88.15 g tower, with an experimental efficiency of 15.04. The first simulated natural frequency was 13.748 cycles/time.

04 · EXPERIMENTAL ENGINEERING

Bouncing-Ball Impact Measurement System

Designed an instrumented drop experiment combining vision, force sensing, inertial measurement and digital image correlation to study short-duration impacts.

OpenCVPythonHX711MPU6050DICMATLAB
Technical deep dive +

Vision. Processed 4K/120-fps video frame-by-frame in Python/OpenCV using HSV thresholding and contour tracking. Pixel coordinates were calibrated to physical distance; velocity and acceleration were obtained through finite differences.

Sensors. Integrated a 5 kg load cell with HX711 amplification and Arduino acquisition, plus an MPU6050 IMU on a compliant membrane. Repeated trials quantified variability and impact response across drop heights.

Cross-validation. Combined optical tracking, force measurement, accelerometry and DIC because each method captures a different part of the same dynamic event. Vision-based impact-speed errors were approximately 8.2–11.4% versus theory.

05 · IMAGE PROCESSING + SCIENTIFIC SOFTWARE

Graphical Data Rectification Pipeline

Developed a multidisciplinary C–MATLAB–LabVIEW pipeline that converts graph images into calibrated numerical traces, rectifies perspective and automatically fits mathematical models.

CMATLABLabVIEWImage ProcessingRMSE Model Selection
Technical deep dive +

A C program parsed binary pixmaps, built grayscale histograms, detected corner and trace intensities, dynamically allocated image data and exported coordinates with explicit error handling. MATLAB ordered corners, computed and applied the geometric transformation, converted pixels into physical units, and selected between sinusoidal and cubic-polynomial fits using RMSE. LabVIEW orchestrated the complete workflow and generated outputs.
